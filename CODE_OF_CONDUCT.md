# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in our
community a harassment free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body
size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender
identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic
status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or
sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming,
diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment for our
community include:

- Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people.
- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences.
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback.
- Accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by our mistakes,
  and learning from the experience.
- Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the
  overall community.

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

- The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances
  of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political
  attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email
  address, without their explicit permission.
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards
of acceptable behavior and will take appropriate and fair corrective action
in response to any behavior that they deem inappropriate, threatening,
offensive, or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or
reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions
that are not aligned to this Code of Conduct, and will communicate reasons
for moderation decisions when appropriate.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces, and also applies
when an individual is officially representing the community in public spaces.
Examples of representing our community include using an official email
address, posting via an official social media account, or acting as an
appointed representative at an online or offline event.

For this project specifically, the community spaces are the repository and
everything in it, namely issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, and
commit messages, along with correspondence that arises from them and any
conference or teaching setting in which the project is presented.

## Enforcement

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior may be
reported to the community leaders responsible for enforcement at
yimanov@student.uef.fi. All complaints will be reviewed and investigated
promptly and fairly.

All community leaders are obligated to respect the privacy and security of
the reporter of any incident.

## How a report is handled

The project is small, so the process is correspondingly simple. Stating it
explicitly matters more than its length, because a reporter deserves to know
what will happen before they decide to write.

1. **Acknowledgement**, normally within two working days, confirming that a
   human has read the report.
2. **Clarification**, if anything in the report is unclear. You will never be
   asked to justify your discomfort, only to supply facts that help the
   response, such as where the behavior occurred and whether it is ongoing.
3. **Assessment** of what happened, using the record where one exists. A
   public thread is usually recoverable even after an edit, and the project
   will look rather than ask the reporter to prove it.
4. **Decision** and action, under the guidelines below.
5. **Communication** of the outcome to the reporter, and to the person
   reported where action is taken, with the reason.

Two commitments apply throughout. The reporter's identity is not disclosed
to the person reported, or to anyone else, without the reporter's explicit
agreement. And a report is never held against the reporter; retaliation for
a good faith report is itself a serious violation.

## What to include in a report

Any report is better than none, so please do not delay because you are
unsure whether you have enough. Where you can, include:

- What happened, in your own words.
- Where it happened, with a link if the space is public.
- When it happened, and whether it is ongoing.
- Who was involved, including anyone who witnessed it.
- Whether you have already raised it with anyone.
- What outcome you are hoping for. You are not required to have one.

A report about the maintainer goes to the same address, which is imperfect
in a single maintainer project and is stated plainly rather than hidden. If
that is not acceptable to you, a report may instead be sent to the Department
of Physics and Mathematics at the University of Eastern Finland, under whose
academic home the project sits, or raised through the abuse reporting
mechanism of the hosting platform.

## Enforcement Guidelines

Community leaders will follow these Community Impact Guidelines in
determining the consequences for any action they deem in violation of this
Code of Conduct.

### 1. Correction

**Community Impact.** Use of inappropriate language or other behavior deemed
unprofessional or unwelcome in the community.

**Consequence.** A private, written warning from community leaders,
providing clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of
why the behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

### 2. Warning

**Community Impact.** A violation through a single incident or series of
actions.

**Consequence.** A warning with consequences for continued behavior. No
interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with
those enforcing the Code of Conduct, for a specified period of time. This
includes avoiding interactions in community spaces as well as external
channels like social media. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or
permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary Ban

**Community Impact.** A serious violation of community standards, including
sustained inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence.** A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public
communication with the community for a specified period of time. No public or
private interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited
interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, is allowed during this
period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent Ban

**Community Impact.** Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community
standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment of an
individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence.** A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within
the community.

## Technical disagreement is not a conduct matter

A large part of the work here is argument about mathematics and about
software design, and vigorous disagreement is welcome. Saying that an
algorithm is wrong, that a complexity claim does not hold, that an interface
is confusing, or that a proof sketch has a gap is exactly the kind of
contribution the project wants, and it is never a conduct violation however
firmly it is put.

What separates it from a violation is the target and the manner. Criticize
the code, the claim, or the design, and support the criticism with a
reference, a counterexample, or a test. Do not characterize the person who
wrote it, their competence, or their motives. Both of the following reject
the same change, and only one of them belongs here.

- Acceptable: this is not associative, here is a counterexample on three
  elements, so the structure is a magma and not a semigroup.
- Not acceptable: anyone who thinks this is associative should not be
  writing algebra code.

When a technical thread becomes heated, a maintainer may ask for it to be
summarized and continued in a fresh issue. That is a moderation step aimed
at the discussion rather than a sanction aimed at a participant.

## A note on language

Contributors come from many places and most of us are writing in a second or
third language. Read a terse message as terse rather than as rude, ask what
someone meant before concluding that they meant something unkind, and accept
that a missing please is usually a missing word rather than a slight.
